# Ilham Aliyev

**Role:** President of Azerbaijan (since 2003)

## Profile
Aliyev has ruled Azerbaijan since succeeding his father in 2003. He has consolidated power through a combination of hydrocarbon wealth, military success, and authoritarian governance. Azerbaijan's victory in the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war and the 2023 dissolution of the self-declared republic dramatically strengthened his domestic position.

## Key Positions
- **Nagorno-Karabakh:** Considers the issue resolved following the 2023 military operation. Over 100,000 ethnic Armenians fled the region.
- **Peace Treaty:** Has committed to a comprehensive peace treaty with Armenia but insists on terms that reflect Azerbaijan's military victory, including transit rights through southern Armenia (Zangezur corridor). Since his June 2026 re-election, Aliyev has maintained demands beyond constitutional change: return of former Azerbaijani enclaves within Armenia, territorial acquisition via border delimitation, and the long-running "Western Azerbaijan" campaign for resettlement of Azerbaijanis displaced from Armenia in the 1990s.
- **"Western Azerbaijan" Campaign:** This is an ongoing, state-directed irredentist campaign, not a post-2026 development. The Community of Western Azerbaijan (WAC) was reactivated by Aliyev's government in 2022 and is directly coordinated by the Presidential Administration (OC Media, Nov 2025). A high-profile international conference was held in Baku in December 2023, and the WAC has since organized events in Germany, Brussels, and at the UN. The WAC appealed to US VP Vance ahead of his Baku visit in February 2026 (OC Media, Feb 2026). The campaign calls for the "return" of 300,000+ Azerbaijanis displaced from Armenia in the early 1990s, framing Armenia's territory as "Western Azerbaijan." On June 24, 2026, the Baku Initiative Group (government-linked NGO) hosted a "Right of Return and Self-Determination" conference in the US Congress — the first event of its kind in the building, featuring personal stories of expulsion from Armenia (OC Media, June 2026). The campaign has been described as "simply irredentist" by Carnegie Europe analyst Thomas de Waal (2023).
- **Turkey Alliance:** Maintains a close strategic partnership with Turkey, which was instrumental in Azerbaijan's 2020 military victory.
- **Balancing Act:** Has cautiously welcomed the US-brokered TRIPP corridor while maintaining relations with Russia. Analysts note Aliyev is in no rush to sign a peace deal, preferring to press for additional concessions as Pashinyan accepts existing demands.

## Military Position
Azerbaijan's military is significantly more powerful than Armenia's, a gap that widened after the 2020 war. Azerbaijan controls strategic positions inside internationally recognized Armenian territory along the border. This military superiority gives Aliyev significant leverage in peace negotiations.

## Key Relationships
|- **Nikol Pashinyan (Armenia):** Negotiating partner. Aliyev holds the stronger hand militarily and territorially. On July 25, Pashinyan held his first call with Putin since the elections — Putin also spoke separately with Aliyev, but neither readout mentioned Armenia.
|- **Recep Tayyip Erdoğan (Turkey):** Close strategic ally. The Turkey-Azerbaijan axis is a cornerstone of Baku's foreign policy.
|- **Vladimir Putin (Russia):** Relations are pragmatic. Russia is Azerbaijan's largest arms supplier, but Aliyev has diversified partnerships. Russian media ran a smear campaign against Azerbaijan in July 2026, but Azerbaijani FM Bayramov still visited Moscow for official talks.
|- **Donald Trump (US):** Engaged through the TRIPP corridor framework. Aliyev cautiously welcomed the US-brokered deal. However, on July 22, the US House Foreign Affairs Committee approved H.R. 9087 (Sherman bill), which Azerbaijan's Milli Majlis condemned as a product of Armenian lobbying.
