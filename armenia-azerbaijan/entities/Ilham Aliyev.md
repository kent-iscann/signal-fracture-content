# Ilham Aliyev

**Role:** President of Azerbaijan (since 2003)

## Profile
Aliyev has ruled Azerbaijan since succeeding his father in 2003. He has consolidated power through a combination of hydrocarbon wealth, military success, and authoritarian governance. Azerbaijan's victory in the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war and the 2023 dissolution of the self-declared republic dramatically strengthened his domestic position.

## Key Positions
- **Nagorno-Karabakh:** Considers the issue resolved following the 2023 military operation. Over 100,000 ethnic Armenians fled the region.
- **Peace Treaty:** Has committed to a comprehensive peace treaty with Armenia but insists on terms that reflect Azerbaijan's military victory, including transit rights through southern Armenia (Zangezur corridor). Post-election (June 2026), Aliyev has escalated additional preconditions beyond constitutional change: return of former Azerbaijani enclaves within Armenia, territorial acquisition via border "delimitation/demarcation," and resettlement of 300,000 Azerbaijanis under the "Western Azerbaijan" campaign. These demands have been elevated to the UN level with appeals circulated to all member states. A "Western Azerbaijan" conference was held in the halls of the US Congress on June 24, 2026.
- **"Western Azerbaijan" Campaign:** Aliyev has formed the "Community of Western Azerbaijan" organization and escalated the demand to resettle 300,000 Azerbaijanis in Armenia to the UN level (appeals circulated to all UN member states in January and March 2023, renewed in 2026). A conference promoting this narrative was held in the halls of the US Congress on June 24, 2026. The campaign reframes the entire territorial question in a way that justifies Azerbaijani revisionism.
- **Turkey Alliance:** Maintains a close strategic partnership with Turkey, which was instrumental in Azerbaijan's 2020 military victory.
- **Balancing Act:** Has cautiously welcomed the US-brokered TRIPP corridor while maintaining relations with Russia. Analysts note Aliyev is in no rush to sign a peace deal, preferring to press for additional concessions as Pashinyan accepts existing demands.

## Military Position
Azerbaijan's military is significantly more powerful than Armenia's, a gap that widened after the 2020 war. Azerbaijan controls strategic positions inside internationally recognized Armenian territory along the border. This military superiority gives Aliyev significant leverage in peace negotiations.

## Key Relationships
- **Nikol Pashinyan (Armenia):** Negotiating partner. Aliyev holds the stronger hand militarily and territorially.
- **Recep Tayyip Erdoğan (Turkey):** Close strategic ally. The Turkey-Azerbaijan axis is a cornerstone of Baku's foreign policy.
- **Vladimir Putin (Russia):** Relations are pragmatic. Russia is Azerbaijan's largest arms supplier, but Aliyev has diversified partnerships.
- **Donald Trump (US):** Engaged through the TRIPP corridor framework. Aliyev cautiously welcomed the US-brokered deal.
