# Nikol Pashinyan

**Role:** Prime Minister of Armenia (since 2018)
**Party:** Civil Contract

## Profile
Pashinyan came to power in 2018 through the Velvet Revolution, a peaceful protest movement that ousted the long-ruling Republican Party. A former journalist and opposition figure, he positioned himself as a reformer committed to anti-corruption, democratic governance, and a foreign policy pivot toward the West.

## Key Positions
- **Peace with Azerbaijan:** Has consistently signaled willingness to recognize Azerbaijani sovereignty over Nagorno-Karabakh in exchange for security guarantees and a comprehensive peace treaty. This stance is deeply unpopular among many Armenians who view it as capitulation.
- **Western Pivot:** Has systematically distanced Armenia from Russia, hosting EU and Ukrainian leaders, signing strategic partnerships with the US, and pursuing normalization with Turkey.
- **Domocratic Reform:** Has pledged to reform Armenia's institutions, including controversial plans to reform the Armenian Apostolic Church's governance.

## Political Situation
Following the June 2026 parliamentary elections, Pashinyan's Civil Contract party won 49.81% of votes — enough for a parliamentary majority but short of the two-thirds needed for constitutional reform. The election was marked by documented Russian interference aimed at bolstering pro-Russian opposition parties. Pashinyan overcame a pressure campaign by Moscow (trade bans, ambassador recall, EAEU referendum demand) and won a mandate to continue peace talks with Azerbaijan and the Western pivot. Opposition leader Samvel Karapetyan (Strong Armenia, 23.29%) is under house arrest in Russia. Turnout was 58%. Trump personally endorsed Pashinyan on Truth Social ahead of the vote.

## Post-Election Outlook
Pashinyan's June 7 victory at 49.81% fell short of the two-thirds majority needed for constitutional reform. Carnegie analysts (June 11) argue this narrower mandate makes a visible peace dividend imperative, not an excuse for delay. The upcoming July 2026 NATO summit in Ankara is identified as a critical diplomatic window — Carnegie proposes Erdoğan convene Trump-Pashinyan-Aliyev on the sidelines. **Aliyev has escalated additional preconditions** post-election — including demands for resettlement of 300,000 Azerbaijanis in Armenia, return of former Azerbaijani enclaves, and territorial adjustments — far beyond the constitutional reform originally flagged as the primary blocker. Deputy FM Kostanyan announced a **2030 target** for full border reopening (June 24), formally pushing normalization years beyond the treaty horizon. Pashinyan met with Zelensky in Yerevan and Security Council Secretary Grigoryan attended the Ukraine recovery conference in Gdansk (June 2026), drawing Kremlin condemnation.

## Key Relationships
|- **Ilham Aliyev (Azerbaijan):** Negotiating partner for peace treaty. Relations remain tense but both sides have committed to a diplomatic process. However, Aliyev has escalated additional demands post-election — including resettlement of 300,000 Azerbaijanis in Armenia and territorial adjustments via border "delimitation." Azerbaijan has begun supplying Armenia with oil/fuel and enabling wheat transit from Kazakhstan/Russia through its territory.
|- **Vladimir Putin (Russia):** Relations have deteriorated sharply. Putin has publicly warned Pashinyan against the Western pivot, comparing it to Ukraine's path before 2014. Russia's concession regime on Armenian railways continues until 2038, limiting Yerevan's independent railway strategy. On July 25, Pashinyan held his first phone call with Putin since the June elections — raising Russian import restrictions as a violation of EAEU rules, while Putin reiterated the demand for an EU referendum. Pashinyan countered that a referendum is only possible after Armenia formally applies for EU membership.
- **Recep Tayyip Erdoğan (Turkey):** Normalization process ongoing. Trade resumed in May 2026 after 33 years. The Akhalkalaki-Kars railway opened for Armenian trade on May 24, 2026. Border remains closed but Armenia has set 2030 as target for full reopening. Turkish VP Cevdet Yılmaz made highest-level visit to Armenia since 2008. NATO summit in Ankara (July 2026) seen as key diplomatic moment.
|- **Donald Trump (US):** Trump brokered the August 2025 peace framework. The TRUMP Route for International Peace and Prosperity (TRIPP) corridor is a key US-backed initiative. DFC has established TDC as a 74/26% US-Armenia joint venture for implementation. In early August 2026, Pashinyan and FM Mirzoyan met **Konstantin Sokolov** (recently appointed head of the TRIPP+ Enterprise Fund) and Aryeh Lightstone (senior adviser to US special envoy Steve Witkoff) in Yerevan.

## Military Reforms
Following the Republic Day 2026 parade, Pashinyan announced a fundamental shift in Armenian military doctrine: the army's sole mission is defense of internationally recognized sovereign territory, with no operations outside borders except international peacekeeping missions. This shift enabled access to arms from seven countries. Armenia's defense industry received 170 billion drams in government investment since 2022. New fortified defense zones have been constructed along Armenia's borders. Eagle Partner 2026 (June 17–25) expanded to a multinational format with US, French, and Greek forces for the first time. Armenia participated in Eurosatory 2026 defense exhibition in Paris with 30 domestic defense companies; BooTech signed a cooperation agreement with French defense firm Thales.
