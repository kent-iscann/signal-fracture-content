# South America's Rightward Shift

Continental realignment from Pink Tide left-wing dominance to conservative governance. As of mid-2026: Argentina (Milei's LLA with multi-decade parliamentary control), Chile (right-centrist plurality in lower house), Ecuador (conservative gains), Colombia (right-wing president elected — Abelardo de la Espriella, pro-Trump businessman, topped polls ahead of runoff), Bolivia (first right-wing president in decades), Peru (Keiko Fujimori won June 2026 presidential runoff on law-and-order platform, vowing military deployment in prisons and along borders).

Drivers: inflation crises, organized crime, migration pressures, public frustration with political elites. Distinctive feature of 2026 wave: Bukele-style strong-arm tactics spreading — leaders modeling themselves on El Salvador's Nayib Bukele, promising rapid security crackdowns with minimal regard for due process.

Risks: rapid austerity and security crackdowns may deepen democratic instability rather than resolve it. The Economist (June 25, 2026) frames this as the "Trumpification of Latin America" — populist right-wing leaders borrowing from the Trump playbook of anti-establishment rhetoric, strongman posturing, and nationalist economic policies.

Brazil remains the major outlier — electorate less rigidly polarized, 2026 election cycle fluid. Peru's statistically tied runoff complicated simple "rightward shift" narrative, underscoring deeply polarized electorates.
