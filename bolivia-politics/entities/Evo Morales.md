# Evo Morales

President of Bolivia 2006-2019. Leader of Movement for Socialism (MAS). Resigned and went into exile in 2019 following contested election. Confined to Chapare region upon return, facing arrest warrant for alleged aggravated human trafficking — linked to allegations he fathered a child with a teenager in 2016 while president. Now carries a second arrest warrant and is officially considered a fugitive.

Remains influential opposition figure but increasingly a beneficiary of events rather than principal organizer. Reduced influence due to weakened regional allies (Venezuela).

In exclusive Reuters interview (June 19, 2026) from his Chapare stronghold, Morales said the surge of popular discontent "made him think" about a return to politics, though he denied actively campaigning. He characterized the protests as an "indigenous rebellion" driven by economic hardship, not his instigation. Issued an ominous warning: "If they don't want it with votes, it's with bullets." Refuses to turn himself in, stating that negotiating his own survival would make him "not worthy."

Paz has blamed Morales for stoking the unrest, though analysts note the government has leaned heavily on portraying Morales as antagonist to distract from underlying social issues.

In a June 18, 2026 CNN interview, Paz escalated rhetoric: warned Morales arrest could come "maybe this weekend" or by end of month; described Morales's support base as "a sect" that defends him "even knowing that the accusation involves his relationship with a 14-year-old minor"; prosecutors seeking 20-year sentence for aggravated human trafficking. Paz's government separately seeking U.S. judicial cooperation to investigate Morales's alleged links to drug trafficking networks. Paz's "turquitos" warning suggested the government believes Morales's protective bubble has informational or logistical vulnerabilities. Paz committed: "Before the year ends, Evo Morales faces justice."

On July 29, 2026, Bolivian prosecutors issued a second arrest warrant for Morales — this time on charges of armed insurrection and terrorism over the 53-day road blockades (May–June 2026) that paralyzed the country. The complaint was filed by the Comité pro Santa Cruz civic group and later joined by the Interior Ministry, alleging Morales's supporters "caused a shortage of food, fuel and medicine" by blocking transport. The Santa Cruz district prosecutor's office specified charges: "armed uprising against the security and sovereignty of the state, terrorism and attacks on the safety of means of transport." Morales responded defiantly from Chapare: "We will continue fighting alongside the people, as we have always done, with the conviction that Bolivia deserves a different path: one of dignity, sovereignty, social justice, and the recovery of hope for all." The warrant also named COB leader Mario Argollo and Tupac Katari leader Vicente Salazar. Morales remains guarded by thousands of armed coca growers in his Chapare stronghold, warning that any attempt to arrest him would be resisted.
