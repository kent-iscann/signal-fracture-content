# Movement for Socialism (MAS)

Bolivia's long-dominant socialist party led by Evo Morales. Governed nearly uninterrupted from 2006-2025. Ousted in 2025 election. Now in opposition but retains significant support among Indigenous and rural working-class constituencies.

Party collapse driven by three-way infighting between Evo Morales, his successor Luis Arce, and Andrónico Rodríguez (young cocalero leader who might have inherited the MAS mantle). This infighting effectively destroyed the party in the months leading up to the August 2025 election.

MAS's fall also reflects a broader crisis of legitimacy: exhaustion of the neo-statist, extractivist economic model dependent on hydrocarbon revenues, widespread corruption, and the party's corporate system of mediation between state and social movements that limited political opposition.

Academic analysis (Bettina Schorr, Verfassungsblog, Aug 24, 2026) argues that the MAS's decline reflects more than organizational collapse — it signals the exhaustion of the "constituent moment" inaugurated in 2009. The MAS was uniquely capable of transforming street mobilizations into electoral success, combining protest with institutional participation. But the 2026 protest wave failed because the party no longer embodied democratic renewal or a shared constitutional project. Instead, the MAS is now associated with economic collapse, institutional deterioration, and the exhaustion of a once-transformative political project. The party's internal struggle between Morales and Arce exposed the absence of institutionalized mechanisms of political succession, transforming a collective project into a contest between rival caudillos.
