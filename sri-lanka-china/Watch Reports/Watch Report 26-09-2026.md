# Watch Report 26-09-2026

## Prediction
Sri Lanka will secure at least one additional yuan-denominated infrastructure financing agreement from China within the next 12 months.

**Probability:** 65% (from 70% — prediction updated)
**Target:** September 2027
**Confidence:** Medium
**Delta Explanation:** The previous prediction (new infrastructure financing agreement within 18 months) has been substantially validated by the Central Expressway cabinet approval and construction resumption. The prediction is updated to reflect the next analytical question: whether the yuan-denominated, smaller-scale, co-financing model becomes the new normal. Probability adjusted from 70% to 65% because the Central Expressway agreement consumed most of Sri Lanka's near-term project pipeline and fiscal headroom for co-financing under IMF constraints is limited.

## What's New
- **Central Expressway: Cabinet approves $500M yuan-denominated loan; construction officially resumes (Sep 17, 2026).** President Dissanayake secured cabinet approval for Sri Lanka's first-ever yuan bilateral loan — $500M equivalent in RMB from China EXIM Bank at 2.5-3.5% interest. Sri Lanka must self-fund $438M from state coffers and settle $200M in outstanding MCC claims. Construction of the Kadawatha-Meerigama section (36% complete since 2020) resumed on Sep 17, now at 42% complete, target completion 2028. Five domestic construction firms urged competitive tenders but Colombo persisted with Chinese contractor MCC.
- **IMF Seventh Review mission (Sep 10-23) ends without staff-level agreement.** IMF Mission Chief Evan Papageorgiou's team visited for nearly two weeks but discussions continue toward a staff-level agreement. The IMF warned that selected clauses in the government's anti-corruption amendments could weaken transparency and accountability. Inflation has returned to 8% (from May's 5.5%). Gross official reserves reached $6.9B (up from $6.5B in June). The IMF reaffirmed commitment to supporting Sri Lanka.
- **Sri Lanka will exit the current IMF EFF program in March 2027.** Minister Bimal Rathnayake confirmed the government intends to complete the four-year program when it expires on March 19, 2027. Two reviews remain (7th and 8th). IMF Mission Chief noted any request for a successor program would be Sri Lanka's decision.
- **New Chinese Ambassador Wei Huaxiang settles in; PRC 77th anniversary reception (Sep 24).** Ambassador Wei presented credentials on Aug 25, met Speaker of Parliament on Sep 10, and delivered his first major speech on Sep 24 at the PRC 77th anniversary reception. He highlighted the Rubber-Rice Pact (75th anniversary in 2027), the 70th anniversary of diplomatic ties in 2027, and announced China's AI-based weather warning system Mazu will be deployed in Sri Lanka. Cultural continuity and policy stability signals.
- **Sri Lanka participates in 11th BRI Summit in Hong Kong (Sep 9-10).** Trade Minister Wasantha Samarasinghe represented Sri Lanka at the 6,000-attendee summit, pitched Sri Lanka as a regional maritime and trade hub to BRI investors. Signals continued high-level engagement with the BRI framework under the Dissanayake administration, with trade and investment diversification as the emphasis.
- **Sri Lanka climbs investor relations rankings; SSGA names it among top 5 EM performers (Q2 2026).** IIF ranked Sri Lanka 4th-fastest improver globally in investor relations (score 43.67/50). SSGA reported Sri Lanka achieved 12.3% total return in USD, contributing 13 bps to EMBI GD index returns — a rare institutional investor perspective confirming market confidence in the post-restructuring recovery.
- **Bilateral debt to China edges up to $5.01B (June 2026), but debt-to-GDP falls.** The Q2 2026 Statistical Debt Bulletin showed China's outstanding bilateral debt at $5.01B (up from $4.974B in Dec 2025). Total government debt fell to 88.8% of GDP from 95% in Dec 2025 — a $5.4B absolute reduction. Total government external debt reached $38.01B.
- **China grants RMB 79M for Cyclone Ditwah bridge reconstruction (Aug 26).** Grant-based assistance (not a loan) to reconstruct 14 bridges. Marks a potential new pattern of grant-based engagement from China in the post-restructuring period.

## Justification

### Political
The most significant political development since August is the conclusion of the diplomatic transition from Ambassador Qi Zhenhong to Wei Huaxiang without disruption. Ambassador Wei's first major speech at the PRC 77th anniversary reception (Sep 24) was a masterclass in continuity: he opened in Sinhala, Tamil, and Arabic, highlighted the Rubber-Rice Pact as a "valuable historical legacy," and explicitly invoked the spirit of "eliminating interference and meeting challenges together" — a direct signal that China expects Sri Lanka to resist external pressure in great-power competition. That he made this statement on the same day that Sri Lanka's forced-labor import ban (July 2026, aligning with US Xinjiang narrative) remains in effect underscores the narrowing policy space for Colombo. So far, Beijing has not publicly retaliated over the ban, but Ambassador Wei's coded reference to external interference suggests private concern.

The Central Expressway construction resumption on Sep 17, personally inaugurated by President Dissanayake, was the most tangible political deliverable of the bilateral relationship since the debt restructuring. Dissanayake publicly thanked President Xi Jinping for his "special attention" to restarting stalled Chinese-funded projects during his January 2025 Beijing visit. This personalizes the success and gives the Dissanayake government a visible achievement to demonstrate that its China engagement strategy is working — important for a government that campaigned on anti-corruption and anti-elite platforms, as Chinese lending is a politically sensitive topic in Sri Lanka.

The absence of an IMF staff-level agreement after the Sep 10-23 mission is a political concern. The IMF's warning that anti-corruption amendments could weaken transparency is notable — the Dissanayake government came to power on an anti-corruption mandate, and IMF questioning of its anti-corruption framework creates domestic political discomfort. The government's stated intention to exit the EFF in March 2027 (confirmed by Minister Bimal Rathnayake) introduces a timeline constraint: with two reviews remaining, any delays in the Seventh Review compress the window for completing the program on schedule.

The BRI Summit participation (Sep 9-10) and the new ambassador's extensive parliamentary diplomacy (meeting with Speaker Jagath Wickramaratne) demonstrate that the bilateral political channel is fully operational. The announcement that China's Mazu AI weather system will be deployed in Sri Lanka — from the ambassador's first major speech — signals that technology cooperation is becoming a new political vector.

### Economic
The Central Expressway yuan loan terms are transformational for understanding the post-restructuring lending model. At 2.5-3.5% interest rate, the cost of borrowing is comparable to the original preferential buyer's credit (which typically carried concessional terms), but the structural shift from USD to RMB carries hidden costs. Sri Lanka has no RMB income and must convert its dollar reserves for repayment. At current exchange rates, rupee depreciation against the dollar and potential RMB appreciation against both the rupee and the dollar could make effective servicing costs substantially higher than the headline rate suggests. The ET analysis explicitly warns of debt-trap risk from this currency mismatch.

The fiscal burden is also higher than headline figures suggest. Sri Lanka is contributing $438M from state coffers — nearly matching the $500M China is providing. When combined with the $200M in outstanding MCC claims the government must settle, Sri Lanka's total outlay related to this project approaches $638M, exceeding China's $500M commitment. This is the inverse of the pre-crisis model where China provided near-full financing.

The IMF mission outcome is economically significant. While the fund called Sri Lanka's economy "resilient" and noted 4.2% growth and $6.9B in reserves, inflation's return to 8% and the stalled review create headwinds. The government's commitment to a 2.3% GDP primary balance target in 2027 now faces pressure from the $438M self-funding requirement for the expressway, which competes with other fiscal priorities. Energy cost-recovery pricing and SOE reform remain IMF conditions.

On the positive side, the SSGA market commentary confirms institutional investor confidence in Sri Lanka's post-restructuring recovery — 12.3% total return in Q2 2026, driven by IMF progress and rating improvements. The IIF investor relations ranking (4th-fastest improver globally) validates the transparency gains. Debt-to-GDP falling to 88.8% from 95% in six months demonstrates genuine fiscal consolidation, though the absolute debt figure ($95B) remains high.

China's RMB 79M bridge reconstruction grant (Aug 26) is a small but symbolically important shift toward grant-based assistance. At ~$11M, it is negligible compared to the $500M loan, but if repeated, it would mark a departure from the loan-heavy model that characterized pre-crisis engagement.

Bilateral debt to China rose to $5.01B (from $4.974B in Dec 2025) despite the restructuring reducing China's share. This marginal increase reflects new disbursements on existing facilities and underscores that even post-restructuring, China remains Sri Lanka's largest bilateral creditor by a wide margin.

The prediction update (65% for one additional yuan-denominated agreement within 12 months) reflects the following logic: the Central Expressway precedent establishes the yuan-denominated model as viable, and several potential projects exist (Hambantota-related investments, Port City Phase II, or energy sector projects). However, Sri Lanka's fiscal headroom for co-financing is limited by IMF program constraints and the $438M expressway commitment. The MSC Hambantota stake — if finalized — could generate revenue streams that improve Sri Lanka's capacity for future co-financing, but this remains speculative.

### Military
No major military-strategic developments since the August report. The MSC Hambantota stake negotiations continue — if concluded, this would strengthen the port's commercial viability and provide operational cover for Chinese dual-use port infrastructure. India's countermoves (Mazagon Dock's Colombo Dockyard acquisition, Trincomalee trilateral, defense cooperation agreement) remain the baseline. China's announcement of the Mazu AI weather system deployment for Sri Lanka introduces a new technological cooperation vector in the strategic domain — weather monitoring systems typically involve satellite data sharing, which could have intelligence-related applications.

### Technological
The Mazu AI weather system deployment, announced by Ambassador Wei at the Sep 24 reception, is the most notable technological development. China's AI-based weather early warning system will be deployed in Sri Lanka, representing the first concrete technology transfer announcement under the new ambassador. This aligns with the CPC's governance model export agenda and the digital cities cooperation dimension flagged in the July CPC anniversary event.

The yuan-denominated loan also carries technological infrastructure implications: servicing RMB debt requires Sri Lanka to develop or access RMB clearing capabilities, potentially accelerating integration into China's Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS). The loan agreement specifically references amendment to the original commercial contract — the first time a Chinese infrastructure loan contract has been restructured with a currency switch, creating a legal and financial template for future yuan-denominated agreements.
