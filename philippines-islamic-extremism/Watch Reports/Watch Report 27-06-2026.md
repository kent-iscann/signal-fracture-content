## Analysis

### Political

The MILF's internal fragmentation has accelerated significantly since the last report. The Bangsamoro Federalist Party is no longer merely a splinter — it has become an institutionalized rival with 1,000+ defectors from UBJP, including a sitting city mayor. The Iqbal corruption scandal (P2.2–4.9 billion in COA-flagged irregularities) has provided the BFP with ammunition to argue that UBJP governance is compromised, while the MILF Central Committee's suspension of Macacua as BIAF Chief of Staff signals that the rift has penetrated the armed wing itself. With 16 parties now contesting the September polls, the electoral landscape is far more fragmented than anticipated.

The Save Bangsamoro Movement's protest at Mendiola (June 15) adds a civil-society dimension — the appeal to Marcos to "restore the leadership" of BARMM implies dissatisfaction with Manila's handling of the transition and could be leveraged by the administration to justify greater oversight. COMELEC's firm stance that the election will proceed eliminates the postponement scenario but raises the stakes: a flawed election under these conditions would be more damaging than a delayed one.

### Economic

The magnitude-7.8 earthquake (June 8) has created a compounding humanitarian crisis in regions already under state of calamity from the oil crisis and El Niño drought. With 736,400+ people affected and 14 LGUs declaring states of calamity, the BARMM government is now spread across disaster response, economic stabilization, and election preparation simultaneously. The peace dividend narrative — already strained — suffers further when governance cannot deliver basic services during overlapping crises.

The broader Philippine economy faces pressure from the ongoing Hormuz energy shock, which has pushed oil above $100/barrel. Marcos's signaling of openness to Reed Bank joint development with China reflects economic desperation but faces constitutional barriers. BARMM's fiscal autonomy remains limited, with the region heavily dependent on national government allotments that may be squeezed by competing disaster recovery and defense spending needs.

### Military

The MILF-Army joint anti-ISIS front (May 2026) remains a positive development, but MILF fragmentation threatens its sustainability. If Macacua's BIAF suspension leads to factional realignment within the armed wing, the operational cooperation could fray. With 43 clan-feuding incidents in the first four months of 2026 and 92 towns identified as election security concerns, the Philippine military faces a three-front challenge: South China Sea patrols, Mindanao counter-terrorism, and election security deployment for September.

The deployment of 7,991 police personnel for election security is substantial but may be insufficient for the scale of the challenge. The historical pattern of rido violence spiking during election periods, combined with the presence of ASG remnants in Sulu and IS-affiliated cells in Lanao del Sur, creates a complex threat matrix. Violent extremism incidents (40 in Jan–May 2026 vs. 29 in late 2025) are trending upward, and the earthquake's displacement of communities may create new recruitment pools for extremist groups.

### Technological

The UNOCT CT TECH+ workshop in Manila (June 4–5) — a joint effort with INTERPOL and the Philippine Anti-Terrorism Council — signals that the Philippines is engaging with international partners on counter-terrorism technology capacity-building. The focus on public-private partnerships to counter terrorist use of new technologies is relevant to Mindanao, where encrypted messaging platforms continue to facilitate extremist recruitment.

The broader trend of AI-enabled radicalization (flagged by the Tacloban school shooting investigation, where NTF-ELCAC raised concerns about online radicalization pathways) adds urgency. However, the Philippine military's cyber capabilities remain limited relative to the threat, and operational outcomes from the UNOCT workshop have yet to materialize.
