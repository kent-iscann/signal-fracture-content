## Analysis

### Political

The political landscape has undergone a qualitative shift since the last report. What was previously characterized as MILF "fragmentation" has now solidified into an institutionalized dual-party system within BARMM politics. COMELEC's formal designation of UBJP as "dominant majority" and BFP as "dominant minority" transforms the schism from an internal MILF crisis into an electoral structure that Manila can manage and leverage.

Marcos's behavior in late July is the most telling indicator of administration intent. His BARMM visit (July 22) distributed P3.4 billion — 85% of which was BARMM government money, not national funds — while pointedly avoiding any engagement with the CAB implementation agenda or MILF leadership. The Presidential Security Command's last-minute postponement from July 21, without cited threat, raised questions about whether the delay reflected security concerns or political calculus. Five days later, his SONA devoted 40 seconds to the peace process out of 86 minutes — less time than was given to coconut tree planting targets. This is not oversight; it is signal.

The Multisectoral Peace Coalition's rapid mobilization and Sarmiento's conciliatory commitments (July 20 dialogue) represent a counterweight. Sarmiento's pledge to restore the IMT and proceed "in close coordination with the MILF" suggests the peace bureaucracy understands the danger. But whether Sarmiento can deliver against the apparent preferences of Malacañang remains unproven. The Sarmiento-Iqbal counterpart relationship (formalized July 15) provides an institutional channel, but one that exists in tension with the administration's demonstrated preference for aid-over-architecture.

Commander Bravo's threat to return to armed struggle is not rhetorical theater. It is the most explicit warning from the MILF's armed wing since the CAB was signed in 2014, and it reflects the growing perception within MILF ranks that Manila is dismantling the peace deal through neglect and unilateral action. The MILF Central Committee's suspension of Macacua as BIAF Chief of Staff (June 2026) and the subsequent BFP defections have created a situation where the MILF's political and armed wings are under separate, potentially competing leadership — a fragmentation pattern that historically precedes violence in insurgent-to-political transitions.

### Economic

The economic dimensions of the BARMM transition continue to deteriorate in ways that feed both political and security risks. The P3.4 billion convergence program distribution (July 22) was structured overwhelmingly from BARMM's own budget (P2.9B), with only P433M from the national government — a ratio that exposes the fiscal weakness of Manila's commitment. Meanwhile, the UNODC TOCTA 2026 report documents how illicit tobacco trade is funding non-state armed groups, and the Sulu-Celebes maritime crime corridor persists as a revenue stream for ASG remnants and BIFF splinters.

The Hormuz energy shock continues to impose macro-fiscal constraints on Manila, contributing to defense budget pressure and reducing bandwidth for BARMM economic development. The P1.23B fund for education and social protection in BARMM (announced alongside July's convergence program) and the P38.86M housing assistance for former combatants represent genuine investments, but they are dwarfed by the scale of need in a region where 40,000 MILF combatants were to be decommissioned under CAB and reintegration programs remain suspended over OPAPRU failures.

The BARMM Board of Investments reported reaching 75% of its P3.2B investment target by March 2026 (P2.4B in Q1 alone), suggesting private sector confidence exists beneath the political turbulence. But the election period — with its prohibition on public works, hiring, and fund disbursement — will freeze government spending from July 16 through September 29, effectively pausing economic activity during the campaign and immediate post-election period. For a region under state of calamity from overlapping crises (oil, drought, earthquake), this freeze compounds humanitarian stress.

### Military

The military dimension presents the most complex picture. On the tactical level, AFP-WestMinCom operations have achieved remarkable results: the July 5 neutralization of BIFF-Bungos leader Esmael Abubakar, alongside DI-Hassan leader Emarudin Kulaw, DI-Maute leader Amerol Mangoranca, and CTG's Anthony Narvasa, represents the most significant decapitation of terrorist leadership in Mindanao since the 2017 Marawi siege. Combined with the CARES framework operations in Basilan (July 15 onward) and MILF Base Command declarations against DI-MG and NPA (May–June), the counter-terrorism picture appears favorable heading into the election.

However, three structural risks offset these tactical gains:

First, Commander Bravo's threat transforms the MILF from a counter-terrorism partner into a potential adversary. The MILF-Army joint anti-ISIS front forged in Munai (May 23) could unravel if the political rift deepens, removing the most effective ground-level intelligence and interdiction asset against ISIS-affiliated cells.

Second, the Crisis Group's warning about educated cadres forming a loosely coordinated jihadist network that could expand to urban areas represents a qualitative evolution of the threat. The ISIS-claimed Munai ambush (January 23, 4 soldiers killed) and the Dalandang assassination on the first day of the gun ban (July 16) demonstrate that even with leadership decapitation, operational capacity persists at the cell level.

Third, the RSIS Global Terrorism Forecast 2026 confirmation that the Bondi Beach attackers trained in Mindanao (November 2025) and that Davao City functions as an FTF hub reinforces the Philippines' designation as a "terrorism academy in Asia." The porous Sulu-Celebes maritime corridor documented by UNODC facilitates the movement of foreign fighters, weapons, and illicit commodities that fund extremist operations. The 27 areas now under red/orange election concern classification represent a security challenge that the 7,991 deployed PNP personnel may struggle to contain, particularly given the Army's simultaneous commitments to South China Sea patrols and post-earthquake disaster response.

### Technological

Technology trends cut in opposing directions. The UNOCT CT TECH+ workshop (June 4–5) and INTERPOL partnership represent international capacity-building on counter-terrorism technology, and the COMELEC's deployment of 6,500 automated counting machines with contingency units suggests the electoral infrastructure is technically prepared. LENTE (Legal Network for Truthful Elections) has deployed long-term observers monitoring disinformation alongside traditional electoral violations — a recognition that information warfare is a primary risk vector.

On the threat side, the educated cadres jihadist network flagged by Crisis Group implies sophisticated use of encrypted communications and social media for recruitment and coordination, potentially beyond the current capacity of Philippine cyber-monitoring. The UNODC's documentation of the crime-terror nexus in the Sulu-Celebes corridor — where cannabis, cocaine, firearms, and cigarettes move through the same smuggling networks — suggests encrypted coordination platforms facilitate both criminal and extremist logistics.

The broader concern is the intersection of AI-enabled disinformation with the BARMM election. The ANFREL/NDI pre-campaign statement (July 30) flagged voter education as "an immediate priority," particularly given the novelty of the parliamentary system for a population that has never voted under it. If disinformation campaigns exploit this information asymmetry — whether through AI-generated content, manipulated media, or coordinated inauthentic behavior — they could undermine the legitimacy of results regardless of the technical integrity of the voting process.

Critically for the prediction, COMELEC's central role in managing electoral technology — including the 6,500 automated counting machines, their software specifications, and the transmission of results — gives Manila a technical lever over the electoral outcome that can be amplified by the election period restrictions on local government hiring and fund disbursement. Any post-election technical disputes over transmission delays, shading thresholds, or machine malfunctions in BARMM could serve as justification for the BTA extension narrative that MILF autonomy requires closer Manila supervision. This technical dependency is the least visible but potentially most durable instrument of central government influence over the transition.
