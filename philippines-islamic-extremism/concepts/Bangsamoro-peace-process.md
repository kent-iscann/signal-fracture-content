# Bangsamoro Peace Process

The ongoing implementation of the 2014 Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro (CAB) between the MILF and the Philippine government.

## Status
- BARMM established in 2019 replacing ARMM
- Transitional government (BTA — Bangsamoro Transition Authority) led by MILF
- First parliamentary elections: September 14, 2026 — the critical near-term milestone
- Government targets exit agreement by 2028 (before Marcos leaves office)
- **June 2026:** Comelec declares "very ready" — 6,500 automated counting machines, 2,393,530 registered voters, 5,212 clustered precincts across 1,186 voting centers. Gun ban July 16–Sept 29. ACM shading threshold proposed to increase from 15% to 25%.
- **June 2026:** COMELEC confirms September 14 election will proceed — no legal basis for further postponement. Campaign period July 30–September 12.
- **June 2026:** PRO-BAR identifies 92 towns and 42 barangays as areas of concern for election security. Up to 7,991 police personnel to be deployed.
- **June 8:** Magnitude-7.8 earthquake offshore Maasim, Sarangani — 65+ dead, 736,400+ affected across BARMM and surrounding regions. Compounds existing state of calamity.
- **May 2026:** BARMM placed under state of calamity (oil crisis + El Niño drought) — compounds pre-election instability.

## Risk factors
- **Spoiler groups:** MILF hardliners, Abu Sayyaf remnants (regaining ground in Sulu per Feb 2026 Patikul encounter), and IS-affiliated cells (Dawla Islamiyah emir killed April 2026) benefit from peace process failure
- **Manila interference:** Marcos administration's oversight posture risks alienating MILF leadership
- **Election violence:** September 2026 elections could trigger clan-based (rido) violence that extremists exploit — CCAA recorded 43 rido incidents in first 4 months of 2026
- **Resource control:** Disputes over Bangsamoro's fiscal autonomy and natural resource revenue sharing with Manila
- **MILF fragmentation:** BFP split from UBJP and Iqbal corruption crisis threaten MILF unity ahead of elections
- **Reintegration failures:** Three of those killed in the April 2026 Dawla Islamiyah operation were former surrenderees, exposing serious flaws in extremist reintegration programs
- **Decommissioning suspended (2026):** MILF suspended BIAF decommissioning citing OPAPRU failures to deliver socioeconomic programmes — a major regression in the normalisation track that risks leaving 40,000 former fighters in limbo.
- **BIFF rebuilding and DIMG recovery:** RSIS CTTA reports BIFF's Salahuddin faction being rebuilt by "Muhammad Usman" in SPMS Box marshlands and DIMG attempting to regroup in Piagapo, Lanao del Sur — both indicators of persistent Islamist recruitment despite diminished capacity.
- **UBJP's narrow electoral base:** RSIS CTTA analysis notes UBJP only represents Maguindanao, not the broader Bangsamoro indigenous population, potentially undermining the legitimacy of MILF governance.
- **Limited female representation:** MindaNews reports only 31.1% of candidates are women; UBJP and BFP have zero women in top 10 party nominee lists, risking governance legitimacy gaps.

## Key developments (July 2026)
- **July 3:** OPAPRU confirms Sulu remains a priority area for peace work under the 1996 Final Peace Agreement with MNLF, despite SC ruling excluding Sulu from BARMM. Bangsamoro government retained funding for Sulu offices.
- **July 3:** Comelec disqualifies UBJP candidate Anwar Alamada (MILF Ad Hoc Joint Action Group chairman) for Maguindanao del Sur 5th District — BFP rival challenged his residency.
- **July 15:** Marcos appoints Mel Sarmiento as chair of Government Peace Implementing Panel for the Bangsamoro, filling the long-vacant post left by Cesar Yano's resignation. Sarmiento retains OPAPRU role. The appointment was a key MILF demand for resuming decommissioning.
- **July 16:** Gun ban takes effect across BARMM (July 16–September 29) for the September 14 parliamentary elections.
- **July 16:** Councilor Nasser Dalandang of Kadayangan town (SGA-BARMM) shot dead on first day of gun ban — rido or election-related violence under investigation.
- **July 18:** Maguindanao del Sur Rep. Esmael "Toto" Mangudadatu defects from UBJP to BFP, joining growing exodus from MILF's political party.
- **July 2026:** In-depth Rappler analysis documents widespread concern over CAB erosion. MILF's Commander Bravo warns the MILF remains prepared to return to armed struggle if the government fails to honor commitments. Peace adviser Sarmiento acknowledges CAB implementation at a "critical phase."
- **July 20:** Multisectoral Peace Coalition (formed after Davao peace summit July 1–3) holds three-hour dialogue with Sarmiento. Sarmiento commits to implementing peace agreement in close coordination with MILF, signals openness to restoring IMT and Third Party Facilitator engagement.
- **July 22:** Marcos distributes P3.4B in projects to BARMM at Camp Siongco — but 85% (P2.9B) from BARMM funds, only P433M national government. No CAB implementation discussion. No MILF leadership meeting.
- **July 23:** BARMM opens Lanao del Norte office — symbolic extension of governance presence outside formal territory.
- **July 27:** SONA 2026: Bangsamoro peace process receives 40 seconds (3 paragraphs) in 86-minute address. No mention of CAB implementation, decommissioning, normalization, or MILF grievances. Civil society groups express disappointment.
- **July 30:** Campaign period opens. 631 candidates for 80 seats. 27 areas under red/orange election concern. Two BFP candidates in Maguindanao del Norte already assured victory (unopposed). ANFREL/NDI deploys joint TAM, flags voter education as priority.
- **July 2026:** COMELEC Resolution 11248 designates UBJP as dominant majority party and BFP as dominant minority party — formalizing the MILF schism as an electoral structure.
- **July 2026:** Crisis Group Report #355: comprehensive diagnosis of peace deal failures. Warns stalled CAB could fuel ISIS activity. Military officer warns educated cadres could form jihadist network expanding to urban areas. Concludes reinvigorated insurgency unlikely but troubling possibility if process falters.

## Assessment
The peace process is at its most fragile point since 2019. CCAA warns violence will likely increase in 2026 as the region heads to its first parliamentary election. Violent extremism incidents rose from 29 (late 2025) to 40 (Jan–May 2026), with deaths nearly doubling. The June 8 magnitude-7.8 earthquake has added a compounding humanitarian crisis that diverts governance bandwidth from election preparation. PRO-BAR has identified 92 towns as areas of concern for election security. Successful elections would consolidate the MILF's political legitimacy and undermine extremist recruitment narratives. Failed or fraudulent elections could reverse years of progress.
- **August 2026:** CCAA 'Boto Mo Bangsamoro!' initiative launched for real-time conflict monitoring during election period. COMELEC 95% ready for Sept 14 polls. 611 violent incidents recorded Jan-July (235 deaths) — rising political violence despite declining aggregate violence. Election campaign period faces twin challenge: extremist threats (twin bombings, Panenekelan Group) and convergence with BSK (Nov 2) — two elections in same season creating compounded risk.
