# Priority Sources — Georgian Geopolitical Position

These sources should be checked first during every source monitor run, before conducting broader Tavily searches. They are organized by category reflecting source value and trust level.

---

## Regional Specialists (high trust)

These outlets provide dedicated, independent coverage of the South Caucasus. Check each for new articles.

- **OC Media** — https://oc-media.org
  Independent journalism covering Armenia, Azerbaijan, and Georgia. High analytical value.
- **Eurasianet** — https://eurasianet.org/region/georgia
  Specialized regional coverage with strong analytical depth. Covers economic developments and Russian influence.
- **Radio Free Europe / Radio Liberty (RFE/RL)** — https://www.rferl.org/Georgia
  Independent news with strong original reporting on the South Caucasus. Covers official statements, political developments, and human rights. Also check their Georgian service.
- **JAMnews** — https://jam-news.net
  Regional news outlet covering Armenia, Azerbaijan, and Georgia. Focus on societal and political developments.
- **Caucasus Watch** — https://caucasuswatch.de/en/news
  Germany-based outlet providing English-language coverage of South Caucasus geopolitics, security, and energy.

---

## Think Tanks & Research Institutions (analytical depth)

These sources provide longer-form analysis, policy recommendations, and original research. Useful for context and deepening understanding, not for breaking news.

- **Carnegie Europe / Carnegie Endowment** — https://carnegieendowment.org and https://carnegieeurope.eu
  Thomas de Waal, Alper Coşkun, and Garo Paylan produce high-quality analysis on the South Caucasus peace process, Russian influence, and regional connectivity.
- **Atlantic Council** — https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/region/the-caucasus/
  Policy-oriented analysis on Caucasus geopolitics, NATO, energy security, and US interests.
- **Caspian Policy Center** — https://www.caspianpolicy.org
  Washington-based think tank focused on the Caspian and South Caucasus. Covers energy, security, and economic integration.

---

## Official Sources (primary — biased, monitor for policy statements)

Direct statements from governments. Essential for tracking policy positions, preconditions, and diplomatic signaling. Treat as primary sources — the framing is always self-serving.

- **President of Georgia** — https://president.spl.ge/
  Official statements and press releases, although worth noting that the Prime Minister of Georgia holds the real power as head of government.
- **Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Georgia** — https://mfa.gov.ge/en
  Official diplomatic statements.
- **Ministry of Finance of Georgia** - https://www.mof.ge/en/
  Official statements regarding the country's financial position, including deals with international financial institutions.
- **Prime Minister of Georgia** — https://www.gov.ge/index.php?lang_id=ENG
  Kobakhidze's government announcements, policy statements, and reform initiatives.
- **Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Russia** — https://www.mid.ru
  Russia's official position on Georgia.

---

## Advocacy & Diaspora (useful signal — biased framing)

These sources represent specific constituencies. Useful for tracking advocacy pressure, Congressional actions, and diaspora mobilization. Always cross-reference with regional specialists.

- **Civil Georgia** — https://civil.ge
  Reasonably independent publication covering Georgia and registered in Georgia.

---

## Source Selection Guidance

1. **Check regional specialists first** — visit/extract each for new articles since the last run
2. **Then check official sources** — scan for new policy statements, demands, or diplomatic signals
3. **Then check think tanks** — for analytical context on recent developments
4. **Then check advocacy sources** — for Congressional tracking and diaspora mobilization
5. **Finally, run Tavily searches** — to catch anything the curated sources missed
