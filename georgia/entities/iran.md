# Iran

Iran has emerged as a surprisingly important and growing actor in Georgia's geopolitical landscape in 2025-2026, representing a new vector in Georgian Dream's multi-directional foreign policy and a growing concern for Washington.

## Expanding influence

Iran's influence in Georgia has expanded systematically across multiple dimensions:

- **Economic:** Nearly 13,000 Iranian companies are registered in Georgia — many to phantom addresses, with hundreds sharing single residential buildings in remote villages showing no physical trace of business activity. RFE/RL's Georgian Service documented this in an October 2025 investigation. The pattern suggests widespread use of Georgia for Iranian sanctions evasion.
- **Political:** Georgia's deputy foreign minister signaled solidarity with Tehran at high levels in 2025. The Georgian government has been reluctant to criticize Iran or support Western pressure campaigns.
- **Media/Cultural:** Tehran is expanding religious and political influence through business networks and cultural diplomacy in Georgia.
- **Government backing:** When the Hudson Institute published a report documenting Iranian influence in Georgia in March 2026, PM Kobakhidze suggested its co-author (former lawmaker Giorgi Kandelaki) might face legal consequences — framing it as a matter for "the relevant state agency."

## Western alarm

The Hudson Institute report stated Iranian activity "directly threatens US national security interests in the South Caucasus, undermines Western influence, and strengthens a regime that is committed to exporting the ideology of the 1979 Islamic Revolution." US lawmakers view Georgia's growing Iran ties as part of a broader pattern of Tbilisi's drift toward adversarial states (Russia, China, and now Iran).

## Strategic implications

Iran's growing presence in Georgia represents:
1. A new sanctions evasion route that complicates Western pressure on Tehran
2. An additional vector of authoritarian influence in Georgia's domestic politics
3. A further strain on US-Georgia relations at a time when Washington is attempting a reset
4. Evidence that GD's "multi-vector" foreign policy increasingly prioritizes transactional ties with adversarial powers over Western partnerships
