# Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC)

The DRC is the epicenter of China's Southern African mineral strategy. The country holds the world's largest cobalt reserves (roughly 70% of global supply) and significant copper, coltan, and lithium deposits. Chinese firms — primarily through the CMOC Group, China Molybdenum, and various state-backed entities — control an estimated 70% of the DRC's mineral exports. The vast majority of these exports are routed to China for processing. China's infrastructure investments in the DRC (roads, railways, ports) are explicitly tied to mining concessions under the "infrastructure-for-resources" model. The DRC is also a key node in the planned Lobito Corridor, which would give China-backed operations an Atlantic export route.

In June 2026, the Kamoa-Kakula copper smelter — Africa's largest copper smelting facility — became operational, processing mine concentrate into blister copper. Chinese mining giant Zijin Mining holds a 39.6% stake in the project, marking a major step in local mineral upgrading in the DRC. The Manono lithium project is also emerging as a new frontier beyond the DRC's traditional copper-cobalt focus, with significant exploration upside remaining as the country's mineral reserves are still considered underestimated by industry consultants.

## Notable developments (2026)

### Glencore stake sale to US/Gulf consortium
In 2026, Glencore sold 40% stakes in select DRC copper-cobalt assets to a US- and Gulf-backed consortium valued at $8–9 billion. This represents the first significant non-Chinese capital injection into DRC's cobalt sector in years, though Glencore retains operational control. The diversification suggests limited appetite among Western firms for majority operational risk in the DRC, and does not fundamentally challenge China's dominant position in the country's mineral export chain.

### DRC reserves and exploration potential
SRK Consulting (June 2026) reports DRC's mineral reserves remain underestimated with significant exploration upside. Beyond traditional copper-cobalt, the Manono lithium project is emerging as a new frontier. DRC is transitioning from state-led to internationally financed mining development, creating opportunities for new entrants alongside established Chinese operators.
