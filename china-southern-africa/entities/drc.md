# Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC)

The DRC is the epicenter of China's Southern African mineral strategy. The country holds the world's largest cobalt reserves (roughly 70% of global supply) and significant copper, coltan, and lithium deposits. Chinese firms — primarily through the CMOC Group, China Molybdenum, and various state-backed entities — control an estimated 70% of the DRC's mineral exports. The vast majority of these exports are routed to China for processing. China's infrastructure investments in the DRC (roads, railways, ports) are explicitly tied to mining concessions under the "infrastructure-for-resources" model. The DRC is also a key node in the planned Lobito Corridor, which would give China-backed operations an Atlantic export route.

In June 2026, the Kamoa-Kakula copper smelter — Africa's largest copper smelting facility — became operational, processing mine concentrate into blister copper. Chinese mining giant Zijin Mining holds a 39.6% stake in the project, marking a major step in local mineral upgrading in the DRC. The Manono lithium project is also emerging as a new frontier beyond the DRC's traditional copper-cobalt focus, with significant exploration upside remaining as the country's mineral reserves are still considered underestimated by industry consultants.

## Notable developments (2026)

### OECD Chinese mine count data
In 2026, the OECD counted 79 African mines owned by or involving Chinese interests, including 30 in the DRC (the highest of any African country). Of the 38 mines in DRC and Zambia, 35 produce copper and/or cobalt. This data, compiled by Ecofin Agency (Sept 2026), provides the most comprehensive cross-country benchmark of Chinese mine control on the continent.

## Notable developments (2026)

### Glencore stake sale to US/Gulf consortium
In 2026, Glencore sold 40% stakes in select DRC copper-cobalt assets to a US- and Gulf-backed consortium valued at $8–9 billion. This represents the first significant non-Chinese capital injection into DRC's cobalt sector in years, though Glencore retains operational control. The diversification suggests limited appetite among Western firms for majority operational risk in the DRC, and does not fundamentally challenge China's dominant position in the country's mineral export chain.

### DRC reserves and exploration potential
SRK Consulting (June 2026) reports DRC's mineral reserves remain underestimated with significant exploration upside. Beyond traditional copper-cobalt, the Manono lithium project is emerging as a new frontier. DRC is transitioning from state-led to internationally financed mining development, creating opportunities for new entrants alongside established Chinese operators.

### CRS Report data (August 2026)
The Congressional Research Service R49080 report provides authoritative data: Africa supplied 99.9% of China's cobalt imports, 91% of chromium imports, and 86% of natural graphite imports in 2025. China also imported 79% of its uranium/thorium, 78% of aluminum, and 81% of manganese from Africa. Total PRC FDI in African mining reached $10.1B (23% of total PRC FDI in Africa). From 2006-2025, PRC firms invested $57.4B in 122 metal-sector projects worth ≥$95M (AEI China Global Investment Tracker data). The DRC accounted for 32% of these largest deals — the largest single-country share. A 2025 US-DRC Strategic Partnership Agreement seeks to counter this dominance. The US DFC has committed $553M for the Lobito Corridor Angola portion and $1B for DRC rail upgrades. The report notes CMOC's surge in DRC copper/cobalt production drove global cobalt prices downward and prompted DRC cobalt export controls in 2025.

### DRC cobalt export controls and pricing leverage
The DRC demonstrated its pricing leverage through a sequence of export controls beginning in February 2025 with a raw cobalt export ban, replaced in September 2025 by a quota system capping exports at 96,600 tonnes — under half of the previous year's production — backed by a strategic reserve empowered to withhold or release volumes. Cobalt responded by approaching $58,000/tonne in early 2026, more than triple the level a year before. In June 2026, the DRC withdrew unused cobalt export quotas and reassigned them to a state-controlled entity, further tightening government control over trading and value addition. KCC (Glencore 70%, Gécamines 30%) produced 188,700 tonnes of copper and 22,900 tonnes of cobalt in 2025. The DRC produces ~76% of the world's mined cobalt (USGS 2025). The DRC/Zambia copperbelt combined produced 4.2M tonnes of copper in 2025 and is identified by the IEA as the single largest source of new copper supply through 2035. However, the window to convert price leverage into lasting industrial capacity is not indefinite: battery manufacturers are moving toward lower-cobalt or cobalt-free chemistries to cut cost and supply risk.
