# Mozambique

> Indian Ocean export gateway; emerging critical minerals and gas frontier; new strategic partner under Xi-Chapo "infrastructure-for-resources" framework.

## Key Facts

- **Capital:** Maputo
- **Key ports:** Maputo (major Indian Ocean gateway), Beira, Nacala
- **Natural resources:** >5 trillion m³ natural gas (Rovuma Basin), graphite, lithium, rare earth elements, coal, titanium, heavy mineral sands (ilmenite, rutile, zircon)
- **Chinese actors:** Hongji Mining Co. (rare earth exploration, Tanzania-linked), China Railway (potential TAZARA/regional rail), Jinan Yuxiao Group (heavy sands mining, DH Mining graphite), DH Mining (subsidiary of Jinan Yuxiao, Nipepe graphite plant), multiple firms via China-Africa Geoscience Cooperation Centre
- **Key development:** April 2026 Xi-Chapo agreement — comprehensive infrastructure-for-resources pact with security component

## China-Mozambique Strategic Agreement (April 2026)

In April 2026, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Mozambican President Daniel Chapo signed a landmark strategic agreement in Beijing — the first state visit by a Mozambican president in 10 years (David Shinn, GWU). The agreement is structured as an integrated package:

### Resource Access
- >5 trillion m³ natural gas reserves in the Rovuma Basin
- Largely untapped graphite, lithium, and rare earth deposits across northern provinces
- China commits to large-scale geological surveys using the China-Africa Geoscience Cooperation Centre and Belt and Road International Geoscience Education and Training Centre

### Infrastructure & Industrialization
- China commits to develop local processing infrastructure to reduce raw material exports and promote industrialization
- Xi stated China is ready to "explore new avenues of cooperation in infrastructure and the integrated development of the energy and mining sectors"

### Security Component
- Beijing commits to support counterterrorism efforts in Cabo Delgado province
- Cabo Delgado insurgency (active since 2017) has displaced >1 million people and disrupted gas and mining projects
- Security support includes training, equipment provision, and joint exercises

### Agricultural Access
- Facilitation of Mozambican agricultural exports to Chinese market through preferential access, reduced tariffs, and dedicated logistics channels
- Cooperation in irrigation, seeds, and transport

### Contrast with Western Approach
- US DFC opted to convert a $31M loan into equity in Syrah Resources (operator of Balama graphite mine) plus $15M investment in its local subsidiary
- China's integrated package (resource mapping, financing, infrastructure, industrialization, security) contrasts with more targeted Western project-by-project approach

## Analysis

The Mozambique-China agreement represents an extension of the infrastructure-for-resources model into the security domain, combining resource mapping, infrastructure, industrialization, and counterterrorism into a single framework. Mozambique is positioned as a new frontier for Chinese resource access in Southern Africa, with both gas (for energy markets) and critical minerals (graphite, lithium, rare earths) as strategic targets. The agreement places Mozambique at the center of international competition for gas and critical minerals.

## Chinese Graphite & Heavy Sands Operations

Two major Chinese mining operations are now active in Mozambique:

### DH Mining Nipepe Graphite Plant (Niassa Province)
- Inaugurated **January 31, 2026** by President Chapo — 200,000 metric tons/year capacity
- Subsidiary of China's **Jinan Yuxiao Group**; $200M investment with 25-year operational timeline
- Built 110km of roads, a bridge across the Lúrio River, and 100km of high-voltage power lines
- Employs ~1,000 permanent and 200 temporary workers, targeting 2,000 in phase two
- Created two competing graphite ecosystems within 500km: Nipepe (Chinese supply chains) vs Syrah Balama (US DFC-backed, Western supply chains)
- China controls ~75% of global natural graphite production, 99% of spherical graphite processing

### Jinan Yuxiao Heavy Sands Operations (Zambezia Province)
- Mozambique Heavy Sand Company VIII (part-owned by Jinan Yuxiao Group) holds >500,000 hectares in mining concessions
- Targets heavy mineral sands yielding titanium ores (ilmenite, rutile) — with ~10x value uplift on processing occurring abroad
- China is the leading buyer of titanium feedstock from Mozambique
- **August 2026:** Mongabay investigation links rapid mangrove loss in Zambezia delta to sand mining — one of the most productive river delta systems globally
- Mozambique's 2026 mining law overhaul (restricting raw mineral exports) does **not** apply retroactively to existing Chinese-held licenses
