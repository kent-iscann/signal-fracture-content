# Tanzania

|Tanzania serves as a key Indian Ocean export gateway for landlocked mineral-rich nations (DRC, Zambia, Zimbabwe). Approximately 70% of DRC's exports to China pass through the Port of Dar es Salaam. The TAZARA railway (1,860km, Zambia to Dar es Salaam), originally built by China in the 1970s and recently rehabilitated, is a critical mineral logistics artery. Tanzania is also emerging as a rare earth producer — in May 2026, a Chinese firm confirmed significant neodymium and praseodymium deposits in the Njombe Region. In June 2026, Chinese company Hongji Mining Co. Ltd partnered with the Tanzanian government for exploration at Mkiu Village, Ludewa District. Tanzania also holds graphite, nickel, and helium reserves critical to clean energy supply chains. Tanzania has also signed agreements linking its ports to the Lobito Corridor vision, creating transcontinental Atlantic-Indian Ocean mineral routes.

## Strategic minerals policy shift (2026)
Tanzania's strategic minerals agreement represents a structural change in resource governance. The government is embedding expectations around local processing, state participation, and regulatory oversight — going beyond royalty adjustments to redefine the extraction architecture. Infrastructure (energy, transport) is positioned as the operational foundation enabling local beneficiation mandates. This forms part of a broader continental renegotiation wave alongside DRC, Zimbabwe, and Namibia.

### US-backed competition in Tanzania's nickel sector
In August 2026, US-backed investment consortium Orion CMC ($1.8B pool, supported by DFC and ADQ) is reportedly close to acquiring a stake in the Kabanga nickel-cobalt-copper project in Tanzania, according to Bloomberg. This would mark one of the largest US-aligned critical mineral investments in Tanzania, directly competing with Chinese-backed mineral extraction in the country. Separately, US-backed Orion CMC was reported planning a $500M investment in a future nickel mine in Tanzania (Ecofin Agency, Sept 2026).

## Shenghe Resources Heavy Mineral Sands Processing Hub (September 2026)
Chinese company Shenghe Resources has commenced construction of a large-scale Mineral Separation Plant in Tajiri, Pangani District, Tanga Region. The plant will serve as Shenghe's regional processing hub for Africa — raw materials from its heavy mineral sands projects in Mozambique, South Africa, and Madagascar will be transported to Tanzania for processing and value addition. Tanzania's Minister for Minerals Anthony Mavunde welcomed the investment as aligned with Tanzania's Mining Act beneficiation requirements. This represents a new model of Chinese mineral processing consolidation in East Africa, positioning Tanzania as a regional processing hub rather than merely a raw material exporter.

## China Geological Survey Cooperation (September 2026)
On September 11, 2026, Minister for Minerals Anthony Mavunde met China's Deputy Minister for Natural Resources and China Geological Survey (CGS) President Dr. Xu Dachun in Tianjin. China agreed to provide grant funding for a high-resolution airborne geophysical survey of Tanzania's Central Block (Dodoma, Tabora, Singida, Shinyanga regions), alongside geological training for Tanzanian specialists, mineral exploration data interpretation capacity building, mining technology exchange, and mineral licensing system strengthening. Mavunde stated the government is prioritising geological exploration under President Hassan's directive to increase mining's contribution to Tanzania's economy. Xu stressed that Tanzania has mineral investment opportunities while China has technology and capital, forming the basis for cooperation. This follows Shenghe Resources' Pangani processing plant as the second Chinese mineral sector initiative in Tanzania in September 2026 and represents Chinese expansion from downstream processing into upstream exploration support.
