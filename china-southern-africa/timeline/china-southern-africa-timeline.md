# Timeline: Chinese Infrastructure Plays in Southern Africa

## 2019
- **Pre-2019:** China's presence in Southern African mining grows steadily through BRI-era infrastructure loans, with Chinese state-backed firms acquiring stakes in cobalt, copper, and lithium operations across the Copperbelt and Zimbabwe.

## 2022
- **Mid-2020s:** China controls an estimated 70% of DRC's mineral exports, with the majority routed through Dar es Salaam (Tanzania) and other Indian Ocean ports. Chinese firms dominate cobalt processing globally.

## 2023
- **2023:** Zimbabwe begins signaling intent to ban raw lithium concentrate exports, demanding that Chinese mining companies invest in domestic processing and beneficiation.

## 2024
- **2024:** Zimbabwe formally bans export of raw lithium concentrates and unbeneficiated minerals. Chinese firms begin committing capital for local processing plants.
- **2024:** China-backed Lobito Corridor project gains momentum — a planned 1,600km rail link from mineral-rich DRC/Zambia Copperbelt to the Atlantic port of Lobito in Angola, designed to bypass traditional South African and Tanzanian export routes.

## 2025
- **2025:** Chinese lithium miners (Sinomine Resource, Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt, Chengxin Lithium) invest ~$1 billion in lithium sulphate processing plants in Zimbabwe.
- **2025:** China becomes Africa's largest trading partner with bilateral trade exceeding $300 billion. Chinese firms operate 78 commercial ports across 32 African countries.
- **2025:** US-China competition for African critical minerals intensifies. US secures Greenland rare earth deal (Tanbreez project) partly to reduce dependence on African/Chinese supply chains.

## 2026
- **April 2026:** Zimbabwe exports its first shipment of lithium sulphate from the Arcadia mine near Harare — the first such product from both Zimbabwe and the African continent.
- **April 2026:** Two Chinese firms (Chengxin Lithium, Sinomine Resource) granted lithium export quotas by Zimbabwe.
- **May 2026:** Zimbabwe's Mines Secretary publicly demands Chinese investors build beneficiation plants domestically — lithium processing, platinum smelting, diamond polishing.
- **May 2026:** Chatham House report notes China's BRI infrastructure (Lobito Corridor, TAZARA railway) has created entrenched critical mineral supply chains that Western nations cannot easily replicate.
- **May 2026:** Chinese firm confirms rare earth discovery (neodymium, praseodymium) in Tanzania's Njombe Region.
- **June 2026:** Flooding in western Angola suspends rail traffic along the Lobito Corridor, disrupting a key export route for critical minerals from DRC/Zambia.
- **June 2026:** EU launches first-ever investment roadshow in South Africa, announcing €12B package under the 2025 EU-South Africa Clean Trade and Investment Partnership. South Africa demands beneficiation as condition for mineral access.
- **June 2026:** Chinese company Hongji Mining Co. Ltd partners with Tanzanian government for rare earth exploration at Mkiu Village, Ludewa District, Njombe Region. Tanzania confirmed as holder of graphite, nickel, helium, and rare earth reserves.
- **June 2026:** Kamoa-Kakula copper smelter — Africa's largest copper smelting facility — becomes operational in DRC, processing concentrate from the mine into blister copper. Chinese mining giant Zijin Mining holds a 39.6% stake.
- **June 2026:** Africa Center for Strategic Studies reports China accounts for ~22% of all African trade, with Chinese firms operating/financing/holding stakes in ~1/3 of African ports. New shipping corridors link African ports to Chinese hubs (Qingdao, Tianjin, Yantai) under universal duty-free access policy.
- **June 2026:** SRK Consulting reports DRC's mineral reserves still underestimated with significant exploration upside. Manono lithium project emerges as new frontier beyond traditional copper-cobalt focus.
- **May 2026:** AidData publishes DRC Chinese Development Finance Profile (2000-2023): 169 grant commitments, $22 billion via 112 loans — confirming DRC as China's largest African development finance destination.
- **May 2026:** ZCCM-IH signs $30M JV with China's Wonderful Group to revive Ndola Lime (600 tonnes/day plant + planned cement facility) — Chinese expansion into industrial processing infrastructure in Zambia.
- **May 2026:** CGTN confirms zero-tariff treatment now covers 100% of tariff lines for all 53 African countries; first shipment (Egyptian oranges) cleared at Shanghai Waigaoqiao.
- **June 2026:** African Security Analysis reports Tanzania's strategic minerals agreement represents structural shift in resource governance — mandating local processing, state participation, and infrastructure-policy integration rather than simple royalty adjustments.
- **June 2026:** Zimbabwe's Finance Minister Mthuli Ncube announces talks with China Railway on resource-backed infrastructure financing at the World Economic Forum in Dalian. The model — using mineral wealth as collateral for transport infrastructure — is explicitly modeled on DRC's $7 billion Sicomines copper/cobalt arrangement with Chinese companies.
- **June 2026:** Bikita Minerals confirms a $400M investment programme to shift from lithium concentrate exports to lithium precursor chemicals; the first phase of its lithium sulphate plant (60,000 tonnes/year capacity) is scheduled for Q2 2027.
- **June 2026:** Mutapa Energy Minerals (a subsidiary of Zimbabwe's state-owned Mutapa Investment Fund) partners with Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt and Tsingshan Holding Group to develop a lithium concentrate processing plant at Sandawana Mine — bringing a new major Chinese industrial group (Tsingshan) into Zimbabwe's lithium sector.
- **June 2026:** Al Jazeera reports Zimbabwe's Q1 2026 mineral sales reached $983.85M, with export volumes up 27% and values up 79% following the raw lithium export ban — evidence that beneficiation policy is driving revenue growth.
- **June 2026:** At the 16th BRICS National Security Advisors meeting, Chinese Foreign Affairs chief Wang Yi calls for deeper BRICS cooperation on strategic minerals, energy security, and supply chain resilience — positioning BRICS as a counterweight to G7 efforts to cut China reliance below 60% for rare earths and permanent magnets by 2030.
- **June 2026:** US Millennium Challenge Corporation expands $491M agriculture compact with Zambia to support critical minerals infrastructure along the Lobito Corridor — signalling US policy becoming more infrastructure-focused.
- **June 2026:** China's State Council releases National Human Rights Action Plan (2026-2030) encouraging human rights due diligence for overseas operations; CCCMC opens call for inputs into its Sustainable Mining Code.
- **June 2026:** G7 leaders adopt Critical Minerals Action Plan at Evian summit (June 17, 2026) targeting supply chain security.
- **June 2026:** US secures preliminary deal with Kenya for access to the Mrima Hill rare earth and niobium deposit, valued at $62.4 billion.
- **July 2026:** Namibia's President Nandi-Ndaitwah holds Namibia-China Business Forum in Guangzhou, courting Chinese investment in mining, renewable energy, logistics, and infrastructure. Husab uranium mine (CGN) highlighted as one of largest Chinese investments in Africa.
- **July 2026:** ECDPM analysis (Jones/Hameiri) reveals Lobito Corridor Western spending figures are inflated: actual EU support under €112M; US $550M railway loan not yet disbursed. 80-95% of copper on Lobito line is China-bound; consortium ~11% Chinese-owned. Zambian spur now costed at $4-5B and "almost certainly will not be built."
- **July 2026:** Xi Jinping hosts Namibian President Nandi-Ndaitwah in Beijing; China and Namibia sign eight documents including green minerals cooperation and economic partnership framework. Joint statement commits to collaboration in uranium, lithium, and rare earths with emphasis on local processing.
- **July 2026:** South Africa opens its freight rail network to private train operators (May 2026) — the country's biggest rail reform in decades, affecting Africa's largest freight rail network (20,000+ km).
- **July 2026:** Glencore sells 40% stakes in select DRC copper-cobalt assets to a US- and Gulf-backed consortium valued at US$8–9 billion, diversifying control without relinquishing operational influence.
- **July 21, 2026:** Angola secures $900M Chinese private investment to construct and operate the Barra do Dande Port Terminal in Bengo province (25-year build-operate concession) — representing Chinese expansion into privately financed port infrastructure operations.
- **July 23, 2026:** Mutapa Energy Resources (Zimbabwe) secures $300M from Chinese entities including Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt and Tsingshan Holding Group to develop the Sandawana lithium deposit with full mine and processing facilities — confirming a deal previously in negotiation.
- **July 26, 2026:** Al Jazeera reports Huayou's Prospect Lithium Zimbabwe lithium carbonate plant is ~90% complete; Huayou has generated >$1.1bn in foreign exchange for Zimbabwe. Government targets manufacturing lithium batteries and solar panels locally.
- **July 28, 2026:** Japan Times reports Zimbabwe designates 13 additional critical minerals — including cobalt, platinum group metals (palladium, rhodium, ruthenium, iridium, osmium), and rare earth elements — banned from raw export effective start of 2027. Over $1B in investments since lithium export freeze.
- **July 28, 2026:** US backs Madagascar rare earths project (CNBC Africa), marking a shift where leading African rare earth developments receive Western rather than Chinese investment.
