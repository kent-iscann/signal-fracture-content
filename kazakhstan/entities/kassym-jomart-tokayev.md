# Kassym-Jomart Tokayev

President of Kazakhstan since June 2019, succeeding Nursultan Nazarbayev. Tokayev has pursued a reform agenda while maintaining the authoritarian structures of his predecessor.

## Key positions
- **President of Kazakhstan** (2019–present)
- **Chairman of the Security Council** (2022–present, after removing Nazarbayev)
- **Chairman of the ruling party Amanat** (2022–present)

## Political trajectory
- **2019–2022:** Initially seen as a continuation of Nazarbayev's rule. Consolidated power after the January 2022 protests by removing Nazarbayev from key positions.
- **2022–present:** Publicly distanced from Russia's invasion of Ukraine while maintaining deep economic ties. Pursued multi-vector foreign policy with increased engagement with EU, China, and Gulf states.
- **2025–2026:** Facing the challenge of balancing Russia's sanctions-era pressure with Western engagement and domestic reform demands. Elevating Kazakhstan from "balancer" to "regional organizer."

## Key policies
- Institutional reform ("100 Concrete Steps")
- Digital transformation and AI strategy — declared 2026 the "Year of Digitalization and AI"
- Multi-vector foreign policy diversification
- Tightening sanctions compliance while preserving Russian economic ties
- **Military modernization:** Issued strict two-year deadline for comprehensive armed forces overhaul, including ANKA UAV production deal with Turkey.
- **Human capital development:** Kazakhstan rose 13 places to 42nd/161 in World Bank HCI+ by end of 2025, driven by education and healthcare reforms.
- **National crypto reserve:** Backed development of $500M-$1B national crypto reserve from seized digital assets and state-backed mining.
- **Constitutional reform:** New constitution approved by referendum (87% support, March 15, 2026) takes effect July 1, introducing unicameral Kurultai, vice presidency, and People's Council.
- **Climate cooperation:** Launched cloud-seeding project with UAE covering 911,000 hectares in Turkistan region — first Central Asian country to implement artificial rain technology at scale.
- **Middle power diplomacy:** Hosted Regional Ecological Summit (9 heads of state), informal OTS summit on AI, and positioned Kazakhstan as a convening state for multilateral initiatives.
- **FDI strategy (July 2026):** Kazakhstan targeting $25.5B FDI in 2026 (up from $20B in 2025). 38th Foreign Investors' Council (July 2) with Eni, ExxonMobil, Baker Hughes, Marubeni, EBRD. Emphasis shifting from capital volume to technology transfer, localization, and domestic capability building.
- **EU relations deepening (June 2026):** 19th EU Dialogue Platform chaired by Bektenov (June 11). Trade turnover $45.2B in 2025. Middle Corridor cargo up 69% in first 5 months of 2026. Kazakhstan obtained observer status at Council of Europe Convention 108 (data protection) — first Central Asian country. Ranked 10th worldwide in UN Online Services Index.
- **EU breakthrough (June 2026):** Visited Brussels (June 21-23), secured $12B+ in agreements/MoUs with EU — 50 Airbus jets (€7.1B), critical raw materials partnership, green hydrogen value chain under Global Gateway framework. Met with Šefčovič, Stiprais, Mora (EIB). Joint statement emphasized Trans-Caspian International Transport Route.
- **Trans-Afghan corridor (June 2026):** Visited Kabul to advance Khaf-Herat railway completion and rail/road connections through Afghanistan to South Asian/Gulf seaports.
- **Azerbaijan connectivity (June 2026):** Kazakhstan-Azerbaijan relations at unprecedented level — first direct Baku-Shymkent flight, first Caspian submarine fiber-optic cable (380 km, 400 Tbps), bilateral trade targeting $1B, 4M tons Kazakh oil via BTC pipeline. Kazakhstan-Azerbaijan Direct Investment Fund with Samruk-Kazyna. Tokayev's planned visit to Baku later in 2026 (6th visit; Aliyev has visited KZ 8 times in 5 years).
|- **Uzbekistan financial cooperation (June 2026):** Hosted Saida Mirziyoyeva in Astana. Uzbekistan modeling Tashkent International Financial Center (TIFC) on AIFC. Bilateral trade $4.8B in 2025 (+16.2%); 7 joint projects worth $1.2B launched Nov 2025. Positions Kazakhstan as regional financial hub exporting institutional expertise.
|- **Kurultai election (Aug 23, 2026):** First election to the new unicameral parliament concluded with 74.19% preliminary voter turnout; ruling Adilet party led with ~71-72%. Nikkei Asia (Moriyasu, Aug 26) frames Tokayev's consolidation of power as central to his hedging strategy — "the world's most important hedger bets on stability." All seven parties on ballot were pro-government; opposition excluded, critical journalists faced pressure. Critics: consolidation, not democratization.
|- **G20 balancing test (Aug 24, 2026):** Experts (Akylbayev, Spink) say Tokayev's Dec 2026 G20 invitation from the US will test Kazakhstan's balancing act. Akylbayev: Kazakhstan should present as a "connector state" — move beyond raw resource export to processing, computing infrastructure, higher-value components. Spink: AI may be the sector where Washington is least willing to accept balance — tensions between Pax Silica membership (June) and WAICO/Chinese tech cooperation. JD Vance personally urged Ukraine to halt CPC strikes, protecting Kazakh oil exports and US commercial interests. Kazakhstan generates >60% of Central Asia's GDP and ~75% of region's trade with US.
|- **SCO/Xinhua middle power messaging (Sept 1-2, 2026):** At 26th SCO summit in Bishkek and exclusive Xinhua interview, Tokayev framed Kazakhstan as a "middle power" bridging geopolitical divisions. Proposed International Water Organisation under UN auspices. Core doctrine: "Kazakhstan's future influence will come from connecting, not choosing." Described KZ-China relations at "unprecedentedly high level" extending beyond energy into AI, robotics, digital transformation. Announced Asia-Pacific digital solutions centre in Almaty under UN ESCAP.
|- **FPC amendments (Aug 14, 2026):** Signed targeted amendments to the 2020-2030 Foreign Policy Concept removing remaining Nazarbayev references, adapting to Kurultai constitution, and formalizing the Astana International Forum. Timesca analysis (Aug 21) frames amendments within context of CPC attacks, expanded Western sanctions scrutiny, and the Belfer Center study citing Kazakhstan as an "aspiring middle power."
