# Armenia-Azerbaijan Peace Process: Structural Notes

## The Sequential Condition Chain (as of May 2026)

The peace process between Armenia and Azerbaijan operates on an explicit sequential condition model, publicly outlined by Azerbaijani Ambassador to Turkey Rashad Mammadov (May 19, 2026):

```
Constitutional amendment (remove territorial claims)
    → Peace agreement signing (initialed Aug 2025, not yet formally signed)
        → Border openings (Turkey-Armenia AND Armenia-Azerbaijan simultaneously)
```

**Key implications:**
- Azerbaijan's primary blocker is Armenia's constitution, not the peace treaty text itself
- Turkey explicitly links its border opening to the Armenia-Azerbaijan peace deal (not independent of it)
- The sequence means even if Armenia amends its constitution, the peace treaty must be signed before borders open
- Pashinyan has pledged constitutional changes, but a referendum is expected only AFTER the June 2026 elections

## Domestic Constraints (Armenia)

- Pashinyan's Civil Contract won 49.81% of parliamentary seats (June 7, 2026 election) — majority but short of the 2/3 supermajority needed for constitutional reform
- Opposition: Strong Armenia (Samvel Karapetyan) 23.29%, Armenia alliance (Kocharyan) 9.9%, Prosperous Armenia 4%. Total opposition + pro-Russian bloc ~37%
- Karapetyan is under house arrest in Russia; called elections "shameful." Turnout: 58%
- Pashinyan's narrower mandate makes constitutional referendum harder but Carnegie analysts argue this makes a visible "peace dividend" more urgent, not a reason to delay
- Constitutional referendum expected later in 2026 or 2027; Pashinyan has framed it as the start of Armenia's "Fourth Republic"

## Aliyev's Escalating Preconditions (June 2026)

Post-election, Aliyev has added several new demands beyond constitutional change:
- **Resettlement of 300,000 Azerbaijanis** in Armenia under the "Western Azerbaijan" campaign
- **Return of former Azerbaijani enclaves** within Armenia
- **Territorial adjustments** via border "delimitation and demarcation"
- Azerbaijan formed the "Community of Western Azerbaijan" organization and circulated appeals to all UN member states (January and March 2023)
- A "Western Azerbaijan" conference was held in the US Congress halls on June 24, 2026
- Analysts assess Aliyev is in no rush to sign a peace deal, pressing for additional concessions as Pashinyan accepts existing demands

## Border Reopening Target

- Deputy Foreign Minister Vahan Kostanyan announced in Brussels (June 24, 2026) that Armenia has set **2030** as the target year for full border reopening with Turkey and Azerbaijan
- Certain transport routes could operationalize earlier as regional projects advance

## Key Railway/Transport Developments

- **Akhalkalaki-Kars railway (Georgia-Turkey):** Opened for Armenian trade May 24, 2026. Built as part of Baku-Tbilisi-Kars project. 105 km total (76 km Turkey, 29 km Georgia).
- **Kars-Gyumri railway (direct Turkey-Armenia):** Idle since 1993. Repair work announced by Pashinyan. This is the ONLY rail link directly between Turkey and Armenia.
- **Yeraskh junction:** Under Russian concession management. Pashinyan announced Armenia will repair it anyway.
- **Azerbaijan rail transit to Armenia:** Opened October 2025. By May 2026: 100K tons of grain, 8.5K tons diesel, ~4K tons gasoline transited.
- **TRIPP corridor:** US-Armenia agreement signed May 2026. DFC implementation structure: TDC US (wholly DFC-owned Delaware corp) and TDC (bi-national JV in Armenia, 74% DFC / 26% Armenia). Multimodal scope includes rail, road, oil/gas pipeline, electricity, fiberoptic. World Bank projections: ~$100M/year transport savings, 0.5-1.0% GDP growth, ~3% export growth, thousands of jobs. No DFC funding committed yet — sub-project equity capped at 40%. DFC creation does not obligate participation in sub-projects.
- **NATO summit diplomatic window:** July 2026 NATO summit in Ankara. Carnegie proposes Erdoğan convene Trump-Pashinyan-Aliyev on the sidelines. Recommended first concrete step: opening Turkey-Armenia border for direct trade and third-country nationals.

## Military Doctrine Shift

As of Republic Day 2026, Pashinyan announced:
- Armenian army's sole mission is defense of internationally recognized territory
- No operations outside borders except peacekeeping
- Equipment from 7 countries (up from near-zero before 2022)
- Defense industry: 170B drams invested since 2022
- Fortified border zones constructed along Armenian borders

This shift enabled arms purchases from countries that previously refused (concerned weapons might be used outside recognized territories, i.e., in Nagorno-Karabakh).
